Best Ways to Make Extra Money After 60 in 2026

Retirement does not always mean you want to stop earning. You may want extra spending money, a cushion against rising expenses, or simply something productive to do on your own schedule. The good news is that many income options no longer require a traditional 40-hour workweek.

Here are practical ideas worth investigating, with an emphasis on flexibility and realistic expectations rather than get-rich-quick promises.

1. Remote part-time work

Customer service, scheduling, administrative support and other remote roles can provide predictable income without a daily commute. Look for positions that clearly state the employer, duties, hours and pay.

2. Freelancing

Writing, bookkeeping, graphic work, consulting, proofreading and virtual-assistant services can turn skills you already have into flexible income. Freelancing is especially useful when you want control over how much work you accept.

3. Tutoring or teaching

Experience in math, reading, music, languages, trades or professional subjects can become tutoring income online or locally. You do not necessarily need to build a full business to begin.

4. Driving and delivery work

Rideshare and delivery apps can offer substantial scheduling flexibility. The important number is not gross pay alone: track mileage, fuel, maintenance and other vehicle costs so you know what you are actually earning.

5. Sell things you no longer need

Unused tools, collectibles, electronics, furniture and household items can generate cash while clearing space. Some people expand this into buying and reselling items, but start small until you understand the market.

6. Pet sitting or house sitting

For people who enjoy animals or want local flexible work, pet care, dog walking and house sitting can produce repeat customers without requiring a conventional job schedule.

7. Local services

Handyman work, organizing, errands, lawn care, photography, computer help and other neighborhood services can turn practical experience into income. Word-of-mouth referrals can be particularly valuable.

8. Affiliate marketing

If you already recommend products or services you genuinely use or understand, some companies offer commissions for referrals. Treat this as a business that takes time to build—not guaranteed instant income—and clearly disclose affiliate relationships.

9. Online rewards and small-income programs

Survey, rewards and referral programs generally will not replace a salary, but legitimate ones can provide modest extra cash or gift cards. Never pay a large upfront fee just to gain access to supposed earnings.

10. Build a small online business

A useful website, niche information service, digital resource or online storefront can grow over time. This route usually takes longer than gig work, but it can create something you own rather than simply exchanging each hour for a paycheck.

Which option is best after 60?

Start with what you already have: skills, experience, equipment, contacts and interests. Then decide whether you want predictable income, maximum flexibility, or something you can build over time. Those are different goals, and the best choice depends on which one matters most to you.
